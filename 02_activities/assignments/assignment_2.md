I would classify the sales performance dashboard as a good data visualization because it strikes a clear balance between clarity, visual appeal, and usefulness. The layout is clean and easy to follow, with consistent colours and well-organized sections. This reduces the amount of effort needed to understand what is being shown. In class, we discussed how simple, two-dimensional layouts and geometric elements can help build trust and make data feel more grounded and factual, and this dashboard aligns well with those principles 

The dashboard also seems well-designed for its intended audience. A business user likely wants to quickly compare product categories, spot geographic patterns, and assess customer feedback. The dashboard makes these comparisons straightforward by using clear KPI cards, bar charts, and a minimal but informative map. This reflects the idea that good visualizations should reduce unnecessary cognitive work and highlight the most important insights without overwhelming the viewer

There is also effective use of grouping. For example, the revenue summary cards share similar shapes and formatting, making it easy to immediately understand that they belong together. This kind of visual consistency draws on Gestalt principles, helping viewers process information more efficiently 

on the other hand, A 3D exploded pie chart is a clear example of poor data visualization. Pie charts already require viewers to compare angles and areas, which is difficult to judge accurately. Adding a 3D tilt further distorts the sizes of slices, making some appear larger simply because they are closer to the viewer. This increases extraneous cognitive load, forcing the viewer to mentally correct for perspective instead of focusing on the data itself (

Separating the slices (“exploding” them) adds even more difficulty, since comparisons must be made across different distances and angles, rather than along a shared baseline. This ignores the principle that visualizations should make comparisons as easy as possible for the audience

This visualization could be improved by replacing the 3D exploded pie chart with a horizontal bar chart. A bar chart uses a shared baseline, which allows viewers to compare category values accurately and with less cognitive effort. Removing the 3D effect would eliminate perspective distortion, and keeping the segments together (rather than “exploded”) would reduce visual clutter. Clear labels, consistent colour use, and optionally sorting the bars from largest to smallest would further help the viewer quickly understand the key differences between categories.
